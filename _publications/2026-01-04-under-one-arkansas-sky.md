summary: >-
  A reflection on a grassroots outreach event held in Dardanelle, Arkansas, a small farming town
  that sat on the path of totality for the April 2024 eclipse. Nearly a hundred people took part,
  among them NASA and university scientists, engineers, educators, students and local residents,
  who ran science demonstrations, hosted a star party and carried out a successful eclipse
  observing experiment. The authors argue that encounters of this kind build curiosity and trust
  in a region that federal science programs rarely reach. They recommend that funding agencies
  expand outreach by partnering directly with small towns, working through local libraries, and
  involving scientists who have rural ties themselves.
