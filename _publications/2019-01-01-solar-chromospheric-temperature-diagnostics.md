summary: >-
  The first simultaneous high-resolution look at the chromosphere in optical and millimeter light,
  combining ALMA Band 3 at 3 mm with the IBIS instrument at the Dunn Solar Telescope. Across the
  plage, network and fibrils in the field of view, the millimeter brightness temperature and the
  core width of the hydrogen alpha line track one another closely, which is surprising because the
  millimeter continuum forms in local thermodynamic equilibrium while hydrogen alpha does not.
  Forward modeling with the RH code explains the link: both diagnostics depend on the population
  of the first excited level of hydrogen, which drives millimeter opacity through Balmer continuum
  ionization and sets the hydrogen alpha width through a curve-of-growth effect. That population
  is regulated by downward Lyman alpha flux, which shifts where both diagnostics form. The
  measured spread in millimeter temperatures is also notably narrower than earlier interpretations
  of hydrogen alpha widths assumed.
