summary: >-
  What happens to magnetohydrodynamic waves when they cross a sharp density interface? This paper
  works through the reflection and refraction problem, beginning by correcting an error in a
  foundational 1971 treatment that affected the conservation of wave energy flux. Applied to
  reflection-driven turbulence in the solar wind, where density fluctuations enhance the
  generation of inward-propagating Alfven waves, the corrected model reproduces the ratio of
  inward to outward Alfvenic power reported in several published simulations. Applied to the
  transition region, it shows that the complex field threading that layer converts part of the
  upward Alfven wave flux into fast and slow magnetosonic modes, which dissipate in a narrow
  region and produce a sharp peak in the heating rate. That peak sometimes exceeds the expected
  heating from Alfvenic turbulence by an order of magnitude, and may explain why earlier models
  appeared to need an ad hoc heat source at exactly this height.
