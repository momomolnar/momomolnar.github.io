summary: >-
  Two puzzles about ALMA's view of the chromosphere are examined using a public data set of weak
  network near disk center that pairs IBIS hydrogen alpha observations with ALMA 3 mm data. The
  known correlation between hydrogen alpha line width and millimeter brightness temperature holds,
  but with different slopes for hot and cool regions, both steeper than previously reported, and
  the split persists for the whole observing run. The second puzzle, the near absence of the usual
  three to five minute oscillations in the ALMA data, turns out to be an artifact of observing
  cadence rather than a property of the Sun. Removing the IBIS data taken during ALMA's
  calibration windows makes the two averaged power spectra nearly identical, and maps of power
  integrated over the p-mode band agree well between the instruments, showing the familiar pattern
  of magnetic shadows and halos.
