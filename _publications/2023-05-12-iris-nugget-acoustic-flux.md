summary: >-
  A companion piece written for the IRIS nugget series, explaining why estimates of acoustic heating
  in the chromosphere have stayed inconclusive since the mechanism was first proposed in the 1940s.
  The work combines IRIS spectra in the Mg II h and k window, which sample several heights at once,
  with IBIS observations from the Dunn Solar Telescope, and compares them against the RADYN and
  Bifrost codes as well as semi-empirical models. The largest source of uncertainty turns out to be
  the plasma density at the height where the lines form, because both that height and the density
  there differ sharply between models, and between different solar features within a single
  three-dimensional model. The attenuation of a wave's amplitude by the time it is measured as a
  Doppler shift also depends strongly on the region being modelled. Using the most realistic models
  available, the acoustic flux falls below the roughly 4 kW per square meter needed to sustain the
  low chromosphere, though the spread between modelling approaches is wide enough that the conclusion
  remains model dependent.
