summary: >-
  The Hi-C 2.1 sounding rocket returned five minutes of ultra-high-resolution 172 Angstrom images
  of a plage region, resolving a population of small dynamic features that sit near the noise
  floor in simultaneous SDO/AIA images. Comparing ten of these events against AIA, IRIS and
  hydrogen alpha data identifies them as ejections of material cooler than the corona, resembling
  the dynamic fibrils and chromospheric anemone jets found in earlier work, with some showing
  heated fronts in the IRIS 1400 Angstrom channel. They are about 3 arcseconds wide on average,
  reach heights near 4 arcseconds, travel at roughly 23 km/s and last about six and a half
  minutes. HMI magnetograms show no mixed-polarity neutral lines at their bases, so either
  opposite-polarity flux is present but unresolved, or these ejections are driven by the shock-
  wave mechanism proposed for dynamic fibrils rather than by miniature filament eruptions.
