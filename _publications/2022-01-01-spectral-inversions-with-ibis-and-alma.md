summary: >-
  ALMA measures gas temperature more directly than spectral lines do, which makes it a useful
  check on the temperatures that non-LTE inversions infer. Using IBIS observations of plage in the
  Ca II 8542 and Na I 5896 lines alongside ALMA, this work runs inversions with the STiC code and
  finds two things that matter for reliability: using column mass rather than optical depth as the
  reference height scale gives better atmospheres above the temperature minimum, and treating
  hydrogen ionization out of equilibrium brings inferred chromospheric temperatures closer to the
  ALMA values. Band 3 brightness temperatures run higher than the inversion temperatures near the
  Ca II 8542 core but correlate well with them, while Band 6 correlates with no single layer.
  Feeding the millimeter continuum into the inversions as an extra constraint pushes Band 3
  atmospheres toward a strong upper-atmosphere temperature rise and Band 6 atmospheres toward
  anomalously cool chromospheric regions, pointing to weakly constrained electron densities as the
  root difficulty.
