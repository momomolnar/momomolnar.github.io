summary: >-
  This work weighs the acoustic energy deposited in the chromosphere against the energy it
  radiates away, using non-LTE inversions with the STiC code. IBIS observations in the Na I 5896
  and Ca II 8542 lines, together with IRIS observations in the Mg II h and k lines, yield
  spatially resolved velocity power spectra and cooling rates at several heights, under both quiet
  and active conditions. Snapshots from a three-dimensional radiative magnetohydrodynamic
  simulation are used to calibrate the method's systematic errors, showing that inversions tend to
  underestimate acoustic flux at all chromospheric heights while slightly overestimating radiative
  losses. Even after correcting for those biases, the ratio of acoustic flux to radiative losses
  stays below unity across most of the field of view, particularly in the upper layers. The
  authors also find that photospheric field inclination correlates with low-chromospheric
  radiative losses in plage, evidence that magnetic topology shapes the energy balance.
