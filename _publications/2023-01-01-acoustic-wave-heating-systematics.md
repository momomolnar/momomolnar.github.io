summary: >-
  This paper asks how much of the disagreement over acoustic heating comes from the analysis
  rather than from the Sun. Sit-and-stare ultraviolet spectra from IRIS and optical observations
  from IBIS are interpreted against synthetic observables from the three-dimensional Bifrost
  radiative magnetohydrodynamic model, following waves above the acoustic cutoff from the upper
  photosphere into the chromosphere. The simulations show that internetwork and enhanced network
  regions propagate waves differently enough to change flux estimates materially. The inferred
  wave fluxes are again too small to maintain the chromosphere, and the authors show explicitly
  that different modeling choices applied to the same observations can drive studies of this kind
  to opposite conclusions.
