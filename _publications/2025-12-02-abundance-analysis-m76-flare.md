summary: >-
  Coronal plasma is enriched in elements with a low first ionization potential, and during flares
  that enrichment can shift within minutes, though the physics behind the fractionation is still
  not settled. This study follows those shifts through the eruptive M7.6 flare of September 30,
  2024, combining SPICE and EUI on Solar Orbiter, Hinode/EIS and the Chandrayaan-2 X-ray Solar
  Monitor, instruments that together span a wide temperature range. Hot and cool plasma behave
  differently: the bright post-flare loop tops seen by SPICE keep coronal composition, while the
  hot plasma seen in X-rays falls from coronal toward photospheric composition during the
  impulsive phase. A three-dimensional reconstruction of the active region, with additional X-ray
  observations from STIX, places each instrument's emission in a distinct part of the flaring
  volume. The authors read this as X-ray diagnostics catching hot reconnection outflows mixing
  with chromospheric material as loops are energized and relax in sequence, while the cooler loop
  tops retain coronal material deposited near their apexes.
