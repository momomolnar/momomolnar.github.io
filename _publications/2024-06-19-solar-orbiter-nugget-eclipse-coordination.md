summary: >-
  An account of the observing campaign built around the April 8, 2024 total solar eclipse, organised
  with the Airborne Coronal Emission Surveyor experiment led from the Smithsonian Astrophysical
  Observatory. The campaign paired an airborne Fourier transform spectrometer, flown over Texas along
  the path of totality, with Cryo-NIRSP at DKIST, the Mauna Loa instruments K-Cor and UCoMP,
  Hinode/EIS, GOES-R SUVI and Solar Orbiter. Two modelling groups predicted the coronal topology
  beforehand so that the instruments could be pointed at a large helmet streamer beside an equatorial
  coronal hole, the same source region Parker Solar Probe was sampling from close in. Cloud disrupted
  the ground-based observations on eclipse day, but the instruments ran successfully the following
  day and recovered the targeted structures. The team plans to trace those structures outward into
  the solar wind measured by Parker Solar Probe and Solar Orbiter.
