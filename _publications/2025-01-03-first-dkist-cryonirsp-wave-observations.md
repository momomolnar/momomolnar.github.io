summary: >-
  Cryo-NIRSP at DKIST observes the same infrared Fe XIII line that established the presence of
  low-frequency Alfvenic waves in the corona, but with roughly nine times the spatial resolution,
  thirty times the temporal resolution and better signal to noise than most earlier data. This
  analysis of one-second cadence sit-and-stare observations examines Doppler velocity fluctuations
  in the Fe XIII 1074 nm line, confirming the known waves and opening a new frequency regime. The
  power-law behaviour of the Doppler velocity power spectrum extends to higher frequencies than
  expected, which challenges models of photospherically driven Alfven waves that predict strong
  chromospheric damping should remove high-frequency power. These high-frequency waves carry less
  time-averaged energy per frequency interval than their low-frequency counterparts, and the weak
  coherence between line amplitude and Doppler velocity confirms that the fluctuations are
  incompressible.
